## 0. CANON SUMMARY — Three-Sentence Pitch 【三句话讲清世界观】

**World:** In the Hollowed Lands, grief is a tangible weight that pools and sickens the world (the Grey Drown); a curse-bound guardian called the Devourer has eaten humanity's sorrow for five hundred years, and the matriarchal Sanctum has turned his feeding into a tithe system that hides its own sins. **Magic:** Grief can only be taken if it is *offered or abandoned*, and every meal costs the Keeper memory and feeling — so the Devourer is a starving, half-forgotten man weeping at the heart of the Hollow, and the only person who can hear his grief is the Chosen Maiden, Elowen, whose sanctity cracks his centuries of numbness. **Stakes:** The Sanctum publicly orders her to slay the monster before the Jubilee of the Vow ends; secretly it plans to bind her as the *new* Keeper on its leash — and the true reading of the Founding Vow, which the Sanctum has misread for centuries, offers a third path: the sorrow can be shared, hand to hand, heart to heart.

## A. MAGIC SYSTEM SPECIFICATION 【魔法体系说明书】

### A1. The Substance — Grief (Sorrow)
Grief is real, tangible, and measurable. It appears as a grey shimmer on the air near the dying and the bereaved; it pools in houses where death has been refused (a house "gone grey"); it can be heard by the sanctified as a language. Grief that is *held* (denied, hoarded, weaponized) festers into the **Grey Drown** — a plague of sorrow: villages sink into weeping sickness, people forget themselves, crops sour, the sky weeps. Grief that is *offered or abandoned* can be eaten by the Keeper.

### A2. The Covenant — The Founding Vow 【誓言的精确措辞 — 全书的锚点】
Inscribed in the Old Tongue on the **First Altar** in Velareth's crypt (full text, canonical):

> **"Let one bear all sorrow, so the many may live — and when the one grows weary, let the sorrow pass to willing hands, hand to hand, heart to heart, until the world can hold its own."**

- **The Sanctum's reading (the Lie)** — the single line embroidered on every Chosen's **Vow-Veil**, taught as the whole vow:
  > *"Let one bear all sorrow, so the many may live."*
  The Sanctum preaches that the vow ended there — a permanent curse on a single eternal Keeper. The **Continuation** (the second line) was struck from doctrine. This is the precise "misread" on which the whole plot turns: the vow was always a covenant of *succession and sharing*, never an eternal sentence.
- **The Continuation (the transfer rite)** — the words Elowen must speak to free Aran and take the covenant willingly:
  > *"When the keeper wearies, I take the weight. I am willing. Let it pass."*
  Spoken in the Old Tongue, before witnesses who are also willing (the Remnant), so the grief passes *into many hands*, not one — this is what makes the HEA possible and structurally sound.

### A3. Source, Cost, Limit 【规则三要素】

**SOURCE — where the magic comes from**
- The Keeper's power is the grief itself, bound by the vow. He is not a mage; he is a vessel.
- He can eat only grief that is **offered or abandoned** — ritually offered at a **Weeping Altar** (tithe), or freely released (a mourner's true letting-go). Grief that is held cannot be taken. *This is why the Sanctum had to deliver whole kingdoms: the tithe-shrines manufactured "offering" at scale, and the offering is both the feeding and the cover-up.*

**COST — what every use of the magic pays**
- Each meal costs the Keeper **memory and feeling**. Five centuries of eating have hollowed him: he has forgotten his kingdom, his face, most of his own name; feeling arrives only as pain, because stored grief presses in whenever numbness cracks.
- Starvation has a cost too: when he refuses to eat, grief pools into the world as the **Grey Drown** — the plague is literally his hunger made visible.
- For Elowen (as successor): taking the covenant costs her a normal life, her place in the Sanctum, and the solitary weight of the world's sorrow — *mitigated only by the Continuation's sharing clause*.

**LIMIT — what the magic cannot do**
- He cannot eat held grief — a single person's clenched denial can starve him.
- He cannot compel, glamour, or control anyone; his only "power" is taking what is given.
- Her sanctity is a **key, not a blade**: Elowen can end him *or* free him, but only by speaking the true vow — she has no offensive magic at all.
- Killing the Keeper with the **Mourning Blade** does not end sorrow; it either releases everything at once (the Drown apocalypse) or — as the Sanctum intends — reroutes the grief into the blade's wielder, binding a new Keeper to Velareth.
- The covenant cannot be cut; it can only be *passed*.

### A4. Sanctity (Elowen's gift) — Grief-Hearing 【圣洁=听见悲伤的语言】
Sanctity is the ability to **hear the Old Tongue — the language of grief itself**. It is passive and relational, never a weapon:
- She can hear the Devourer weeping when no one else can hear him at all.
- Contact with her skin cracks his numbness — he *feels* again, and because feeling returns as stored pain, tenderness literally hurts him (the reverse "touch her and die": *touching him makes him remember how to hurt*).
- Only a grief-hearer can speak the Continuation and complete the covenant's true reading.
- The Sanctum knows sanctity marks the *ideal new Keeper* — which is why Elowen was chosen, groomed, and veiled since childhood. They never told her what she was being groomed *for*.

### A5. Magic & Society 【谁有魔法、谁掌权、谁恐惧】
- **Who has it:** one person — the Keeper (Aran); and one key-holder (Elowen). There is no magic school, no spell system. 
- **Who wields it:** the Sanctum, through liturgy and the tithe — they control the Altars, the offerings, the Ledger, and therefore the Keeper's life. Their power is *administrative*, which is why it can be exposed with a ledger, not a lightning bolt.
- **Who fears it:** everyone on the Rim. Villagers fear the Drown, fear the Inquisition, fear the moment a child "hears" too much. The tithe is presented as mercy and experienced as theft of memory.

### A6. Three Worked Examples 【实战示例 — 每次用魔法都必须制造新问题】
1. **The Thornwake tithe (Act 1 context).** Village offers its autumn grief at the Weeping Altar; the shrine-keeper lights the offering; the grief leaves them — they feel lighter, and forget the dead (memory is part of the meal). **New problem:** the village never learned to mourn. The abandoned grief is eaten, but the *meaning* is gone; a dull, wordless sadness settles that no one can name, and the village turns docile to the Sanctum — they have given away the emotional fuel of resistance. The tithe doesn't heal; it anaesthetizes.
2. **Elowen's first touch (romance beat 3, mid-Act 2).** She reaches to comfort the weeping man; her hand on his shoulder — he crumples; centuries of stored grief flood him; he begs her never to touch him again. **New problem:** her compassion is now something she must fear. The very tenderness that could save him is forbidden by the world's rules (his pain, her heresy). Intimacy becomes a moral risk for both of them — which is exactly the book's central engine.
3. **The blade's true purpose (climax, Act 3).** The Matron intends Elowen to use the Mourning Blade — not to kill Aran but to *cut the covenant and bind the grief into Elowen*, the Sanctum's new Keeper on a leash: fed by tithes, silenced forever, the monster in a veil. Elowen instead speaks the Continuation; the grief passes to her **and** to the willing Remnant hands. **New problem:** she now carries the world's sorrow — the book's final cost, borne *shared*; and the Sanctum falls, but Inquisition remnants will hunt her (sequel hook).

## B. FACTION DOSSIERS 【势力档案 — 每个势力都有独立于主角的动机】

### B1. The Sanctum of Velareth (the Temple) — *the true monster*
- **Goal:** institutional survival at any price. Keep the Keeper (or a controllable replacement) fed, blamed, and silent; keep the tithe flowing; keep the doctrine that the Devourer causes sorrow rather than eating it.
- **Resources:** the holy city Velareth; the Weeping Altars across the Rim; the Tithe-Ledger (the smoking gun — five centuries of what they fed him and why); the Inquisition; the Chosen-Maiden program; total control of public liturgy and history.
- **Internal factions:**
  - **The Matron's Circle** (hardline, led by **Matron Virelle**): knows the truth, plans the Jubilee transfer of the covenant into Elowen.
  - **The Reformists** (a handful of sisters, incl. **Sister Celestine**, Elowen's old teacher): suspect the tithe is wrong; the seed of doubt Elowen carries.
  - **The Inquisition** (enforcement, led by **Inquisitor Malvyn**, a Rim-born convert): zealots who hunt heretics; know nothing of the true plan — useful deniability.
- **Attitude toward Elowen:** she is their chosen weapon and vessel — loved as a saint, owned as a tool. The moment she questions, she becomes their heretic.

### B2. The Devourer / Aran, last king of Aranvail — *the Keeper*
- **Goal (surface):** oblivion. He has wanted to die for centuries; he believes he is the monster they say he is. **Goal (buried):** to be seen — to have one person hear him and still not recoil.
- **Resources:** the grief he has eaten (an ocean of it — power he refuses to use); the Hollow (his territory, where the swallowed kingdoms' ruins stand); the Weeping Throne (Aranvail's old hall, where he weeps); his own self-loathing.
- **Internal condition:** he is fractured by eaten memories — three kingdoms of voices surface in him; he is not a saint, but a survivor of his curse: he has genuine rage, hunger, and shame. The Remnant's name for him is **the Mercy**; he hates the name.
- **Attitude toward Elowen:** she is the first warmth in centuries — and therefore his greatest fear, because feeling returns as pain and because his shame insists he will corrupt her.

### B3. The Remnant — *survivors of the three swallowings*
- **Goal:** keep the Mercy alive and fed (they know someone took their grief, and they thank him for it); preserve the Old Tongue; and for the Vengeful wing, destroy the Sanctum.
- **Resources:** hidden enclave **the Stilled Vale** (a valley where grief runs thin); fragments of the Old Tongue and of the full vow; knowledge of the tithe's true history; grief-offerings freely given.
- **Internal factions:**
  - **The Merciful** (led by elder **Kessa**, who remembers Maelthra): want to save the Keeper, restore him, ease his shame.
  - **The Vengeful** (incl. young scholar **Deren**): want to use Elowen as their blade against Velareth.
- **Attitude toward Elowen:** they become her new people — the willing hands the Continuation requires. Their love is what makes the shared burden bearable; their vengeance is what she must soften.

### B4. The Sisterhood of the Veil — *Elowen's family*
- **Goal:** faith — to see the Chosen succeed and the world delivered. They believe the doctrine they were raised on.
- **Resources:** the order's authority, its prayers, its love for Elowen.
- **Internal condition:** they expect a triumphant return. Her heresy will break them — the moral cost of her choice is real, not rhetorical.
- **Attitude toward Elowen:** love plus expectation — the heaviest non-magical weight in the book.

| 0 | **The First Drowning.** A plague of sorrow nearly ends the world. Nine mourners write the Founding Vow; **Aran**, last king of the Green Vale, speaks it and binds himself as the first Keeper. | The Sanctum is founded at Velareth; the First Altar is inscribed with the *full* vow; Aran's people name their land Aranvail in his honor. |
| 47 | The tithe is codified — the **Weeping Altars** rise across the Rim; sorrow is gathered as ritual offering. | Legitimate at first: helping the Keeper feed. The machinery of the cover-up is built. |
| 152 | The **Tithe-Ledger** begins recording what is eaten and why. | The ledger becomes a weapon: the Sanctum learns to bury its own sins in the offerings. |
| 214 | **The First Concealment.** A blight caused by Sanctum mismanagement is fed to the Keeper as "village grief." | Doctrine begins to invert: the Devourer is now blamed for sorrow rather than thanked for eating it. The Remnant's oral histories mark this as the corruption's true birth. |
| 388 | **The Swallowing of Aranvail** (famine the Sanctum failed to relieve; the kingdom's grief is delivered). | Canonized as the **First Deliverance** — a holy feast. Aran's own kingdom is the first to fall. |
| 401 | **The Swallowing of Ceredys** (plague; the Sanctum quarantines the healers, feeds the plague-grief). | **Second Deliverance.** The Drown is held at bay — at the price of a kingdom. |
| 429 | **The Swallowing of Maelthra** (a war the Sanctum fomented between Rim lords to feed him). | **Third Deliverance.** The **Remnant** forms from survivors; the Stilled Vale is founded. |
| 461 | The **Inquisition** is created. | Heresy law hardens: hearing the Old Tongue, touching the Hollow's edge, or refusing a tithe becomes a capital sin. |
| 486 | Elowen is taken from the tithe-village of **Thornwake** — the first grief-hearer born in generations. | She is groomed as the Chosen Maiden, veiled with the truncated vow, taught to aim the Mourning Blade. |
| 500 (present) | **The Jubilee.** The Matron announces the final solution: the Chosen Maiden will enter the Hollow and slay the Devourer before the year's end. | Public: end the monster's tyranny. **Timeline rule for drafting:** Aran is *not* immortal by nature — he has simply been bound so long that eating grief replaced aging. This is why "unmade mortal" is a release, not a demotion: the covenant was always supposed to end.

## E. SOCIETY & LAW 【社会规则 — 平衡当代读者价值观】
- **Gender:** The Sanctum is matriarchal — women hold sacred office, men are barred from the priesthood (the Inquisition is male-led enforcement under female authority). This hierarchy is presented as a *control mechanism the book critiques*, not as virtue: doctrine casts women as "vessels of sorrow" and men as "hounds of order." Elowen's freedom is leaving the cage, not defending it.
- **Marriage:** free and secular on the Rim (handfasting); the Sanctum discourages it and claims first claim on children ("gifts" — how Elowen was taken). The romance's HEA is a marriage *outside* the institution's blessing — deliberately.
- **Inheritance & class:** Rim commons hold land in common; the Sanctum "inherits" grief (tithes) and, increasingly, land through grief-taxes. The economic engine of the oppression is the tithe, not a feudal ladder.
- **Heresy law:** the romance is *structurally transgressive* — every tender moment with the Devourer is a capital sin. Warmth is the rebellion.
- **Death & mourning:** because the tithe eats grief *and memory*, ordinary mourning is dying out on the Rim; the Remnant's practice of *remembering aloud* (keeping grief in the heart, un-offered) is itself a quiet heresy — and the emotional counterweight to the tithe's anaesthesia.

## F. NAMING CONVENTIONS 【命名规范】
- **Sanctum sphere — Latinate (liturgical):** Velareth, Virelle, Celestine, the Chosen, the Orison. Adopted at veiling; chosen names, not birth names.
- **Rim sphere — Celtic/Welsh (earthy, grief-flavored):** Elowen, Kessa, Deren, Thornwake, the Stilled Vale. The Old Tongue is the Rim's pre-Sanctum language — fragments survive only in the Remnant.
- **Swallowed kingdoms — same Celtic stock:** Aranvail ("Aran's vale"), Ceredys, Maelthra.
- **The Devourer's true name:** **Aran** — speaking it aloud is heresy; the Remnant say it only in the Stilled Vale. His title "the Devourer" is Sanctum slander; the Remnant call him **the Mercy**.
- *Rule: no new character or place name may be introduced without checking this table — keep the two circles (Latinate vs Celtic) clean.*
