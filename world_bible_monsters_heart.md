## THE WORLD IN 3 SENTENCES
A grief-plague once nearly drowned the world, and a chant-singer named Auren ended it by speaking a vow — *"Let one bear all sorrow so the many may live"* — becoming the Devourer, a living vessel who must eat the world's grief forever. The Temple of the Unburdened, founded to maintain that vow, discovered it could tithe grief like tax, and for nine centuries it has fed him — with shrines, with whole kingdoms it abandoned, with the sanctified girls it sent to "slay" him. Elowen, the Nineteenth Sancta, walks into his Vault to finish the job and finds, instead, a weeping man — and the half of the vow the Temple erased.

**中文速览：** 世界=「圣殿靠献祭/遗弃的悲痛喂养吞噬者，把瘟疫/战争/饥荒的锅都推给他」的九百年谎言。全书悬念悬于立誓原文的真义；吞噬者只能吃「被献出或被遗弃」的悲痛；艾洛温触碰他会让他重新学会痛；凡人化=他终于能感受的需求实现，不是降级。

## 0. THE VOW — 立誓原文（CANON, FIXED）

### 0.1 The Canonical Line（定稿，不可改动）
> **"Let one bear all sorrow so the many may live."**

This exact string is the book's spine. It appears in-world three times, each disclosure deliberate:
1. **Epigraph / Chantry catechism** (spoken by Elowen, Act 1 — she believes it).
2. **The Vault wall, carved in Old Rhemian** (Act 3 — she sees it as liturgy, not truth).
3. **The Old Velanthi chant, recovered** (climax — she *finishes the sentence*).

### 0.2 The Two Texts
| Version | Language | Text | Who has it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temple gloss (corrupt) | Old Rhemian (liturgical) | *"Unus omnem luctum portet, ut multi vivant."* | The Temple's liturgy, carved on every shrine |
| Original vow (true) | Old Velanthi chant (pre-Temple) | *"Ren vel anen, vel eol."* — **"Let one bear, and the many carry."** | Only the Riverfolk Remnant still sing it, half-forgotten |

### 0.3 The Struck Verse（被抹去的下半句）
The full vow Auren spoke had a second line — the community's answer — which the Temple struck from the liturgy within fifty years of the founding:

> **"…and let the many bear him, when he can bear no more."**

The Temple canonized half a sentence. That is the lie at the foundation: the vow's power came from *exchange* — one offers, the many carry him in turn. The Temple kept the offering and erased the carrying. The Devourer has been waiting nine centuries for someone to finish the sentence.

### 0.4 The True Reading（真义）
- **Temple misreading:** *unus* = the singular sacrificial vessel. "One (the Devourer) must bear all sorrow, forever, so the many (everyone else) may live *unburdened*." This justifies an eternal economy of feeding — and a world that never has to grieve, which is to say a world that never fully feels. The Temple's "peace" is the same numbness as the Devourer's — mirror, not opposite.
- **True reading:** *unus* is distributive — "let each bear their share." The vow is a covenant of *shared* grief: none drowns because all carry. Grief is not a debt to be paid to one; it is a weight to be passed, in bearable portions, among the many.

**Why the plot hangs on it:** Slaying the Devourer (the Temple's solution) validates the misreading — the "many live" *because* one is sacrificed. Freeing him (Elowen's heresy) requires speaking the true vow — which cannot be spoken until she understands that *she* is the "many" who must now bear him. Her final act is not a kill and not a rescue: it is **completing the vow's grammar**.

## A. MAGIC SYSTEM SPECIFICATION 魔法规格

### A1. SOURCE（来源）
**Grief itself.** In this world grief is a real substance — visible as **grief-mist** (grey condensation that beads on cold stone, gathers in still water, and stands in fields of mass sorrow). It is generated by loss, cannot be created or destroyed by any person, and *wants* to be carried. The Devourer is not a god who eats sorrow; he is a **vessel** — a man the vow's literalization turned into a walking container for the world's grief. The magic of this world *is* the vow; everything else (shrines, grey roads, the Vault, the Sorrowblight) is plumbing for that one covenant.

### A2. THE DEVOURER'S RULE（吞噬规则 — 不可违反）
He can consume **only** grief that is:
- **OFFERED** — ritually given: tithes, vows, offerings laid on shrines, the dying's last words entrusted to him, a life consecrated and sent down the Pilgrim's Road. (The Temple's entire mechanism.)
- **ABANDONED** — denied, forgotten, discarded: grief the world refuses to carry. A people declared cursed and left; a mourning rite outlawed; a loss the powerful decree "never happened." (The mechanism that swallowed three kingdoms.)

He **cannot** take **HELD** grief — grief a person actively carries, honors, and refuses to surrender — no matter how vast. This single rule is the mercy and the trap:
- It is why the Temple must *deliver* kingdoms to him (it cannot simply order him to eat).
- It is why the Remnant, who carry their grief in songs and orchards and bells, are the one people he could never consume.
- It is why **Elowen can touch him**: her grief is held. The Temple tithed it from her as a child — except one seed she hid. That held seed is the only thing in the Vault he cannot take, and therefore the only door into him.

### A3. COSTS（代价）
**His cost — every meal costs him (1) memory and (2) feeling.** He has eaten for ~900 years. He no longer remembers his name, his river, or the face he once had; he feels almost nothing — cold, starving, ashamed. Eating is self-erasure; refusing is world-poisoning (see A6). He is a survivor of his curse, not a saint: he has rage, hunger, and a terrible resentment that he is ashamed of. Give him these in every draft — the gentle monster who is *not* gentle inside.

**The world's cost — tithed grief is real grief.** When the Temple takes an offering, the bereaved do not heal; they go *hollow*. The Temple sells numbness as peace. The swallowed kingdoms were eaten wholesale because they were abandoned — the land itself died with the grief (Grey, Silence, Wither). And the Sanctae: each was a *sanctified meal* — a life trained to contain grief, offered. Their grief and their selves were eaten. That is what the Temple calls a heroic death.

**Elowen's cost — tenderness is violence.** Her touch breaches his numbness, which means her love *hurts* him — physically, at the level of the curse. Every tender act is also an act of wounding; she must choose to hurt him by loving him. This is the reverse "touch her and die": **she is the danger to him**, and the price of his feeling is her willing cruelty of care.

### A4. LIMITS（限制）
1. **Offered-or-abandoned only.** Held grief is inviolable — even for him, even starving.
2. **The Vow is his cage.** While the misread vow stands, he cannot leave the Vault; the Vowstone's threshold is the leash. He *must* take what is offered (refusal pools it — see A6).
3. **Numbness is armor and prison.** Sanctified touch cannot crack it unless the toucher is *wholly herself* — not a weapon. Eighteen Sanctae were not; Elowen, whose conditioning cracked, is.
4. **He cannot create or destroy grief** — only consume it, hold it, and (at the end) return it.
5. **No grief, no power.** His immense strength is entirely borrowed from the grief inside him; the less he holds, the smaller and more human he becomes. The HEA's unmaking is thus *physically legible*: as the grief leaves him, the mountain shrinks into a man.

### A5. TITHE-SHRINE MECHANISM 献祭神龛机制
The **tithe-shrines** (public name: *shrines of the Unburdened*) stand in every town of the living world — a grey stone basin under a bell, always cold, always damp with grief-mist. Four steps, airtight:

1. **LAYING-DOWN** — A mourner speaks the Offering Prayer over the basin and places a token: a lock of hair, a letter, a coin, a bell-tongue. The token is the grief made physical.
2. **THE CONDUIT** — The shrine's stone is bound to the Vowstone by the vow's own power. Offered grief flows down the **grey roads** — underground veins of grief-mist that run from every shrine to the Hinge, like veins to a heart. (The Pilgrim's Road is the only *walkable* grey road — the others are under the earth.)
3. **THE FEEDING** — The grief arrives in the Vault and the Devourer must take it, or it pools and rots (A6).
4. **THE RECEIPT** — The **Ledger-Keepers** record every offering in the **Ledger**: the true account of what was tithed, by whom, and what it bought. The Ledger is the Temple's secret history — and the proof of every crime. One Ledger-Keeper has kept a *true* copy.

**Shrine bells:** grief-arithmetic — each death is counted in bell-notes; a bell rings once when an offering is accepted. In the Hollow Kingdoms the bells hang dead. The Remnant keep one bell — the Unsilenced Bell of Kalmar — that rings *against* the system (see B4).

### A6. THE SORROWBLIGHT（未咽之悲）
When grief is not eaten — refused, hidden, or generated faster than it can be tithed — it pools and rots: grey mold on crops, rivers running grey, a weeping-plague that hollows whole villages from the inside. The Temple names it "the Devourer's curse" and blames his hunger. The truth: **it is the world drowning because the Devourer has begun to refuse** — he is ashamed, he has started starving himself, and grief has nowhere to go. This is the ticking clock of Act 1 and the refutation of the Temple's solution: *slaying him does not end the blight — it uncorks the Vault.* The pooled grief of nine centuries floods the world. The only true cure is the vow's second half: the many must carry again.

### A7. ELOWEN'S SANCTITY（圣洁=钥匙，非利刃）
Sanctity in this world is not power over grief; it is *transparency to* grief — the capacity to hear and hold it without being consumed. The Temple trains Sanctae as perfect weapons and perfect tithes, but its conditioning **makes them less transparent**, not more: they hear only the Devourer's roar because they cannot hear the man. Elowen's conditioning cracked young — she kept her mother's seed (held grief), she asked the wrong questions, she loved the wrong people — and so she is the first Sancta who hears, at the Vault's heart, **a man weeping**. Her sanctity is the key that can end him *or* free him, and only the true vow decides which.

### A8. TENDERNESS-AS-DANGER（触碰=重新学会痛 — 与感情线严丝合缝）
The romance mechanic, canonical and non-negotiable:
- His numbness is armor. Her touch — warm, whole, *her* — breaches it. What floods in is not pleasure but **feeling returning all at once**: a century of grief, memory-shards, and pain. Touching her makes him "remember how to hurt." Tenderness is literally his suffering.
- Therefore every romance beat is doubled: closeness is heresy (Temple law) *and* violence (to him). The 6 slow-burn beats from the romance feasibility map onto this exactly:
  1. **A voice** — he weeps in Old Velanthi; she hears a man. (No touch yet.)
  2. **He teaches her to hear grief** — intellectual/spiritual closeness before any touch; he can *sing* grief, show it to her, and this alone costs him.
  3. **First touch = crisis** — her hand on his skin, he crumples, the Grey inside him *moves*; the first shard of his name surfaces.
  4. **Almost-touch** — she reaches, stops; both of them weep. (The world's tenderness at its most dangerous.)
  5. **The transfer begins** — the most intimate act available: giving her his burden. Touch is now *work*, a rite, a vow.
  6. **First kiss = goodbye-gift** — he expects it to complete his ending (the misread vow: one bears all, even unto death). She uses the kiss to *refuse* — to speak the true vow and finish the sentence.
- **His arc:** numbness → pain → joy. Every step of feeling is paid in pain; that is the point. **Her arc:** doctrine → doubt → the willing cruelty of care (choosing to hurt him because it is the only way to free him).

### A9. MORTALITY-AS-FULFILLMENT（凡人化=需求实现，非降级）
When Elowen speaks the true vow, the hoarded grief is **returned to the world in bearable portions** — not erased (grief is real and must be honored), not dumped, but *shared*, as the vow always meant. The Devourer is unmade: the grief that was his body leaves him and the mountain becomes a man — a man who feels everything for the first time in nine centuries: cold, hunger, joy, embarrassment, love. **This is not demotion; it is the fulfillment of his stated want.** From the first page his want is oblivion because feeling is unbearable; the HEA gives him the thing he actually needed — the capacity to feel, *and* a world that will carry him when he can bear no more. The cost is Elowen's: she becomes the **Keeper of Grief** — not a new single vessel (that would repeat the lie), but the first among the many bearers: the one who holds the Ledger of grief, teaches the sharing-rites, and plants her mother's seed in the first green thing to grow in the Wither.

### A10. THREE WORKED EXAMPLES 实战示例
**Example 1 — The River Offering (Act 1, environment + action; Velanth, the Grey).**
Elowen crosses the dead river-road and passes a shattered shrine. A Riverfolk woman kneels at the broken basin, lays a lock of hair on the stone, and speaks the Offering Prayer. The grief-mist on the basin *moves* — grey beads stream toward the Hinge, along the grey roads, into the earth. Elowen realizes with horror: the shrine still works. Nine hundred years after Velanth was swallowed, the Temple still feeds him. *(Demonstrates: offered-grief rule · shrine conduit · the feeding that never stopped.)*

**Example 2 — The Unsilenced Bell (Act 2, dialogue; Kalmar, the Silence).**
The Bellsmiths ring the Unsilenced Bell for their war-dead, and the grief-mist around it stays *bright*, not grey. Elowen: "Why doesn't he take it?" Bellsmith: "He cannot. It is not his. It is ours." *(Demonstrates: held-grief immunity · the rule's mercy · Remnant theology — and plants the vow's true reading in her ear before she ever reaches the Vault.)*

**Example 3 — The First Touch (Act 3, the romance crisis; the Vault).**
Elowen lays her hand on the Devourer's. His armor-cold stillness cracks; a century of grief hits him at once; he crumples and weeps, and a shard of his name surfaces — *Auren*. Tenderness is literal pain; her love is the wound that heals him. She will spend the rest of the book choosing to wound him on purpose. *(Demonstrates: sanctity-as-key · held-grief safety · the transfer-ritual foreshadow — the touch begins the transfer the vow will complete.)*

## B. FACTION DOSSIERS 势力档案

### B1. THE TEMPLE OF THE UNBURDENED（圣殿 — 真正的怪物）
- **Public face:** a white-stone temple-city, **Hallowmere**, on a mirror-still lake. Doctrine: "Bring your grief; leave unburdened." The Devourer is the debt-collector of the world; only the Temple's sanctified maidens can reach him and slay him.
- **True nature:** an economy. The Temple needs the Devourer to exist — the monster justifies the Temple; the tithes keep him fed; the world's dependence keeps the Temple rich, holy, and unaccountable. It has caused nothing, and prevented nothing: it let plagues, wars, and famines run their course and *collected the grief*, then blamed the Devourer.
- **Goal:** maintain the vow as misread, forever. The High Unburdener genuinely believes it — the tithe is the only thing standing between the world and the Drowning. She is a villain of system, not of cackle.
- **Resources:** the shrine network (every town), the **Ledger** (the true account), the **Chantry** (the school that raises Sanctae), the Warden-Sisters (temple-guard), control of written history, grief-tax revenue, the Pilgrimage rite.
- **Internal factions (the schism the book exploits):**
  - **The Ledger-Keepers** — the accountants; they know the truth and serve the system cynically. One of them keeps a *true* copy of the Ledger and will defect when the proof is needed.
  - **The Orthodox Unburdeners** — true believers; the truth shatters them. Some become the book's most dangerous enemies (a believer betrayed is a zealot).
  - **The High Unburthener** — head of the Temple; knows everything; the final gate.
  - **The Sisterhood** — Elowen's fellow Aspirants/Sanctae; her old family; they expect a triumphant return. The emotional cost of her heresy.
- **Attitude to Elowen:** she is their perfect weapon and their finest tithe — until she hears the man.

### B2. THE DEVOURER / AUREN（吞噬者本体）
- **Identity:** Auren, once a Velanthi chant-singer — golden-haired, bright, the one who spoke the vow. Now a mountain of grief in the Vault beneath the Vowstone; the man is a soft-spoken figure at the mountain's heart, weeping in Old Velanthi.
- **Goal at story start:** oblivion. He cooperates with each Sancta's "slaying" — which is why the Pilgrimage keeps working. His want is death; his need is to feel again, joy and pain together.
- **Resources:** nine centuries of grief (immense power), memory (dwindling), his shame. **Territory:** the Vault and the Hollow Kingdoms — the dead zones are his; no one else can survive there; the grief is his body.
- **Independent motive:** hunger vs. mercy. He ate the abandoned; he raged; he is not a saint. He has begun refusing meals — out of shame — and the Sorrowblight is the result. He is simultaneously the world's savior, its scapegoat, and its poisoner.
- **Attitude to Elowen:** she is the first thing in centuries that *hurts* — which is to say, the first thing that is real.

### B3. VELANTH — THE GREY（第一被吞王国 · 瘟疫）
- **Who they were:** the river kingdom — the Vel and its chant-farmers. Grief-culture: the **Chant of the Flood** — grief sung into the river, carried downstream. The vow itself was born from this culture (Auren was theirs).
- **How they fell (AV 612):** the Grey Plague. The Temple declared Aelbridge cursed and **abandoned** it. The abandoned grief of a whole people was eaten at once; the land became the **Grey** — ash-mist that stills rivers, fog that eats sound and color.
- **Remnant:** the **Riverfolk** — survivors who kept the Chant and half-remember the true vow. They are the seed of the Remnant; they are why the Old Velanthi vow survives at all.

### B4. KALMAR — THE SILENCE（第二被吞王国 · 战争）
- **Who they were:** the bell kingdom of the high marches. Grief-culture: **grief-arithmetic** — every death counted in bell-notes; families paid a **grief-tax** (the Fallen Ledger) for the dead. Bells were the public nervous system of grief.
- **How they fell (AV 704):** the **Bells' War**, a succession war the Temple brokered — and then *tithed*. When the losing king refused to pay war-grief as tax, the Temple **abandoned** his people; the eaten grief silenced every bell at once. Kalmar is now the **Silence**: no sound at all — wind makes no noise, bells hang dead.
- **Remnant:** the **Bellsmiths**, who keep the **Unsilenced Bell** ringing in defiance — held grief, the one grief he cannot take. They are the book's proof that the rule is mercy.

### B5. THALYR — THE WITHER（第三被吞王国 · 饥荒）
- **Who they were:** the orchard kingdom. Grief-culture: the **Withering Vigil** — one tree planted per death; orchards as living memory. Grief was gardening.
- **How they fell (AV 801):** the **Long Hunger**, a famine while the Temple's tithe-granaries stayed full. The Temple named the famine the Devourer's doing and **abandoned** Thalyr; the eaten grief withered every tree at once — white skeletons in grey chalk-soil.
- **Remnant:** the **Orchard-keepers**, who still tend the dead orchards, hoping. **Elowen is a Thalyr orphan** — her mother's seed (the one held grief the Temple never took) is her humanity and her key; the final image of the book is that seed, planted in the first green thing in the Wither.

### B6. THE REMNANT（遗民 — 她的新人民）
Survivors of all three Hollow Kingdoms (plus refugees of the blight) who know the truth: *someone took the grief*. They venerate the Devourer as a god of mercy — not because he is kind, but because he carried what they could not. They carry their own grief (songs, bells, orchards) and are therefore the one people he can never eat. They guard the Hinge, keep the old chants, and become Elowen's new family — the "many" who will bear her when she can bear no more.

### B7. RELATION MAP（关系图）
```
                  TEMPLE OF THE UNBURDENED (Hallowmere)
                   │  feeds (tithes/shrines) ──────────►  DEVOURER/AUREN (Vault)
                   │  blames (plague/war/famine)        │  territory: Hollow Kingdoms
                   │  abandons ────────────► VELANTH (Grey) · KALMAR (Silence) · THALYR (Wither)
                   │                                   ▲
                   │  persecutes                      │ venerate / protect (carry their grief)
                   ▼                                 │
                REMNANT ◄───── Elowen joins ──── SISTERHOOD (expects her triumph)
                   │  (Riverfolk · Bellsmiths · Orchard-keepers)
                   └── keeps the true vow / the seed / the Unsilenced Bell
   LEDGER-KEEPERS ── one true copy ──► (the proof that brings the Temple down)
```
**Independence check:** every faction has a goal that is not "help/hurt Elowen." The Temple wants the economy to run; Auren wants oblivion; the Remnant wants the truth honored; the Sisterhood wants their faith vindicated. Elowen's every choice has political consequence in all six directions.

### D4. RULES OF THE WORLD（规则速查）
1. Grief is a substance; cannot be created or destroyed; wants to be carried. (A1)
2. The Devourer eats only **offered or abandoned** grief; held grief is inviolable. (A2)
3. Every meal costs him **memory and feeling**. (A3)
4. The misread vow is his **cage** — he cannot leave the Vault while it stands. (A4)
5. Tithed grief leaves the giver **hollow**, not healed. (A3)
6. Untithed, uneaten grief pools into the **Sorrowblight**. (A6)
7. Her touch makes him **relearn pain**; tenderness is literal danger to him. (A8)
8. Sanctity = transparency to grief; only a whole self cracks his numbness. (A7)
9. The vow's true reading: **grief is shared, not hoarded.** (0.4)
10. Mortality = his need fulfilled, not his power stripped. (A9)

### E2. Romance-Line Integration（与 Character & Romance Designer 的接口）
- All six slow-burn beats are mechanically supported (§A8) — no beat requires bending a rule.
