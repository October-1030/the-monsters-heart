# Romantasy Phase 1 — Worldbuilding Feasibility Pass
**Role:** Worldbuilding Designer | **Date:** 2026-08-12 | **Status:** Ready for Lisa's final direction pick

Based on Story Architect's 3 core concepts (story_concepts.md) and Character & Romance Designer's feasibility pass (romance_feasibility.md). Each entry: magic system (source / cost / limit), political factions (independent motives), self-consistency check (closed logic loops, no conflicts with the romance line).

---

## 1. THE BRIDE OF SHADOWS

**Magic (hard).** *Source:* the Veil, the border-realm of old bargains; the oath-rings are Veil-forged relics, bound when both rings touch during the wedding vows. *Rules:* (1) only **intentional** harm is shared — every shared wound is a choice, never an accident (locks in the romance fix); (2) mortality is **mutual** — if either dies, both die, which is why the immortal finally has a stake and shields her; (3) the bond cannot be cut while both live — the only exit is a Veil-bargain: one partner surrenders something of self (her mortality, his crown). *Cost:* every shared wound feeds the Veil and deepens its claim; the longer the marriage holds, the more the bargain-keepers "remember" the pair — so the costly HEA is contractual, not punitive. *Limit:* Nyra's veil-touched immunity is passive — she cannot be read, compelled, or forgotten, but she can compel nothing either; the rings are the only magic she can touch.

**Politics.** (1) Voss-realm, the famine kingdom — harvests blighted by Veil-rot; its council has sent brides before (all died silently) and does **not** know the bond exists, so Nyra's survival is itself the reveal. (2) The Shadow Court — immortals split: Old Shadows want a controllable king and quietly supply bride-contracts; New Silence wants him deposed. (3) The Veil's bargain-keepers — a power with its own ledger, which is why "surrender" endings exist at all.

**Self-consistency.** Closed loop: desperation explains the contract; the altar rite explains the secret; mutual death explains his protection; the Veil-bargain explains the peace (two rulers share one heartbeat, so war is literally suicide). No conflict with the romance beats — every shared wound is chosen, and every kiss is a negotiation the magic legitimizes.

## 2. THE SUN'S WIDOW

**Magic (low, court-bound).** *Source:* the twin thrones — Sun rites govern truth and oaths; Winter rites govern cold and forgetting. *Cost:* Sun-rites burn the caster's warmth and years; Winter-rites numb the caster's feeling. That cost is the world's gift to the romance: Emrys's legendary coldness is not temperament but tuition — the Butcher of the Frost Line is a man who paid with empathy — so his thaw is him putting down the magic, a choice with military consequences, not a personality fix. *Limit:* truth-rites work only on Sun Court ground and only on oaths the swearer **believes**; the usurpers sealed the king's courts the night he died, so the farce trial is held rite-less — which is why the murder cannot be solved by magic in chapter one. The poison was mundane (a physician's tincture), delivered by a trusted hand.

**Politics.** (1) Sun Court Regent-Council — staged the trial to kill or disgrace her and rule through her son. (2) The Winter King, off-page — the actual poisoner; the marriage is his **legal** conquest: a queen-husband gains the regency over her son without storming walls (sieges are expensive, legitimacy is cheap), and her death would collapse that claim — which is why Emrys can never simply be ordered to kill her. (3) Emrys — the king's instrument who knows the truth; his defection is the political engine. (4) The city and her son — the moral clock.

**Self-consistency.** Siege-plus-marriage is rational; the investigation *is* the courtship because every clue peeled is a mask peeled; the son's unfiltered honesty shatters both performances through world-logic, not convenience. No contradictions with the romance beats.

## 3. THE MONSTER'S HEART

**Magic.** *Source:* grief itself — the Devourer is a curse-bound guardian created by the Temple's founding vow: "let one bear all sorrow so the many may live." That wording must be fixed in the World Bible before drafting; the whole plot hangs on its true reading. *Rules:* he can eat only grief that is **offered or abandoned**, never what is held — which is why kingdoms had to be delivered: the Temple's tithe-shrines collect sorrow as ritual offering, both the feeding and the cover-up. *Cost:* each meal costs him memory and feeling; starvation pools grief into the world as the plague of sorrow. Only Elowen's sanctity cracks the numbness — so touching her makes him "remember how to hurt": tenderness is literally his pain. *Limit:* her sanctity is a key, not a blade — she can end him **or** free him, but only by speaking the true vow the Temple has misread for centuries.

**Politics.** (1) The Temple — the real monster, hiding famine, plague, and war behind "the Devourer's sins." (2) The Devourer — a faction with his own hunger, territory, and starvation. (3) The Remnant — survivors who venerate him as a god of mercy (they know grief was eaten, that someone took it) and become Elowen's new people. (4) Her sisterhood — expecting a triumphant return.

**Self-consistency.** The twist is built into the premise, so every tender moment is heresy by Temple law — the world makes the romance structurally transgressive. The HEA closes: the grief transfers to her as new keeper (foreshadowed by the transfer-ritual beat), and he is unmade mortal — his need fulfilled, not demoted.

## Cross-Concept Verdict (worldbuilding lens)

- **Bride of Shadows:** the Veil-bargain gives the world texture, and the pain-bond is hard magic with clean rules — the strongest force-multiplier. Watch: keep Veil mechanics to three sentences; jargon is the risk.
- **Sun's Widow:** lowest worldbuilding lift, richest courtly texture; magic-cost-as-empathy is the single strongest idea — it makes the romance physically legible on the page.
- **Monster's Heart:** highest emotional ceiling; must stay precise (the vow) to avoid sermon-vagueness; the tithe mechanism is the skeleton — make it airtight.

**Note:** three separate worlds, no shared canon — each gets its own World Bible; only the selected concept is expanded into the full A/B/C/D docs (magic spec, faction dossiers, timeline, World Bible index).
